# Selection by measured utility before adoption

The current hook accepts optional units in order. This can consume the
budget before relevant snippets or tests, but heuristic priority
is not demonstrated benefit. We will experiment with a pure function with weights per
role, explicit distance, declared risk and whole dependencies.

The prototype stays outside the automatic handler. Its input and output are
bounded; explanations stay in the selection receipt so they do not occupy the prompt.
The baseline receives the same candidates and mandatory requirements. Weights and
promotion criteria are frozen before inference. Rejecting the scorer
without a gain in two rounds is a valid conclusion of X1, not proof that all
utility-based selection is infeasible.

We use historical reproductions of two real defects and falsifiers that
fail before and pass after the original fixes. Aider proposes patches;
a bounded executor applies and verifies them. The experiment covers that
implementation flow, not automatic hooks or the complete cycle of every host.
Details and limits are in SPEC-X1 v1.1. The Harness stays frozen.
